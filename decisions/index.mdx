An Architecture Decision Record captures a choice **at the moment it was made**, with the context that made it reasonable and the alternatives that were explicitly rejected. Read later — six months on, by a contributor who wasn't there — an ADR answers the question every codebase eventually raises: *why on earth is it like this?* Without the record, the answer is archaeology. With it, the answer is a paragraph.

The value is in the parts that are uncomfortable to write. A record that lists only benefits is marketing. Every ADR here states what the decision costs, because that is the part that keeps the record honest and useful when circumstances change.

## The format

Every record on this site uses the same four sections, in the same order:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Context">
    The forces in play **before** the decision was made. What made this a question at all? An ADR whose context does not explain why the obvious answer was insufficient is not describing a decision — it is describing a preference.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Decision">
    What was chosen, stated plainly and in the present tense. One paragraph, not a design document.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Consequences">
    Both directions. What this makes easy, and what it makes hard or impossible. **The costs section is what earns the record its place.** A record that acknowledges no costs is one where the costs haven't been found yet.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Alternatives">
    What else was considered, and specifically why each was rejected. Not strawmen — the version of each alternative its advocate would recognise.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
ADRs are **immutable once accepted**. A decision that changes earns a new record that supersedes the old one; the old record stays in place, marked as superseded. Editing an accepted ADR destroys the only thing it existed to provide: the record of what was known and believed at the time the choice was made. The reasoning is the same as the ledger's append-only entries — what happened is separate from what was later thought better.
</Note>
