Amina works in Munich and sends money home to Nairobi every month. Today she is sending €1,000 to her mother's M-Pesa wallet. This page follows that single transfer through every context — naming the service called, the events emitted, and the exact ledger entries produced at each step. It is the same €1,000 used in the [ledger walkthrough](/architecture/ledger#worked-example) and the [settlement saga](/architecture/settlement-saga), assembled end to end so you can see how every piece connects.

## Before she starts

Amina is a **Tier 2** account: passport, proof of address, and selfie verified. Her per-transfer ceiling is €15,000 and all rails are available to her. She holds a EUR virtual account with a German IBAN, funded by a salary transfer.

Her mother is not an Arc customer. She is a **beneficiary** — an M-Pesa MSISDN Amina saved earlier, screened at the moment it was added and screened again on every transfer.

<Note>
Beneficiaries are screened at **use**, not only at creation, because sanctions lists change. A beneficiary who was clear six months ago may not be clear today. Screening once and trusting the result indefinitely would make the list meaningless for anyone added before it was updated.
</Note>

## The flow

```mermaid
sequenceDiagram
    autonumber
    participant A as Amina
    participant P as Product
    participant R as Risk
    participant M as Movement
    participant L as Ledger
    participant C as Chain (Base)
    participant B as M-Pesa

    A->>P: POST /v1/quotes (EUR 1000 → KES)
    P->>M: quote request
    M-->>P: rate, fees, receive amount, 30s expiry
    P-->>A: quote

    A->>P: POST /v1/transfers (quoteId)
    P->>R: screen sender, beneficiary, amount, corridor
    R-->>P: approved (score 12)

    P->>M: execute(quote, beneficiary)
    M->>L: reserve journal
    M->>L: swap journal
    M->>C: broadcast USDC transfer
    C-->>M: confirmed (10 blocks)
    M->>L: settle journal + network fee journal
    M->>B: payout KES 138,254.00
    B-->>M: accepted
    M->>L: payout journal
    M-->>P: transfer.settled
    P-->>A: notification
```

## Step by step

<Steps>
  <Step title="Quote — the price, and its expiry">
    **Service:** Movement · `QuoteEngine.quote`

    Amina requests a quote for EUR 1,000 → KES. The engine prices the corridor in real time:

    ```text
    send             €1,000.00
    corridor fee     45bp + €0.35 fixed   =    €4.85
    network fee      (Base estimate)      =    €0.06
    net                                   =  €995.09
    mid rate         EUR/KES 139.7
    quoted rate      mid − 60bp           =  138.86
    receive          net × quoted rate    =  KES 138,254.00
    fx spread fee    (net × mid) − receive =    €6.06
    ```

    Amina sees **send €1,000.00, receive KES 138,254.00** — with the €4.85 corridor fee and €6.06 FX margin shown as separate line items, not folded into the rate.

    The quote expires in **30 seconds**. Rates move, and honouring a stale quote is an unhedged loss. `assertUsable` throws on an expired quote before any subsequent step can proceed. [The quote that aged →](/stories/the-quote-that-aged)
  </Step>

  <Step title="Compliance — the gate">
    **Service:** Risk · `ScreeningService.screenTransfer`

    Three checks compose into one verdict:

    - **Tier:** €1,000 is well inside her €15,000 Tier 2 ceiling, and mobile money is an allowed rail. Pass.
    - **Sanctions:** sender legal name and beneficiary name screened by Jaro–Winkler against the synthetic list, including aliases. No match above threshold.
    - **AML:** five rule families evaluated against her transaction history. Monthly €1,000 transfers to the same beneficiary on the same corridor score low on velocity, low on structuring, and zero on unusual corridor. Counterparty concentration is high — she sends to one person — but below the minimum count and window thresholds.

    **Verdict:** `approved`, risk score 12, no case opened. A `compliance.decided` event is published carrying the score and an empty reasons array.

    <Note>
    If this step had returned `rejected` or `review`, the saga would stop here — before `reserve`, before any money moves at all. Compliance is not a background check that runs in parallel with funding; it is the gate the saga must pass through first.
    </Note>
  </Step>

  <Step title="Reserve — take the money and the fees">
    **Service:** Movement → Ledger · `kind: transfer`

    The reserve journal debits Amina's virtual account for the full €1,000 and immediately allocates each component to the account that owns it:

    | Account | Dr | Cr |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | `liability.customer.va_amina.EUR` | 1,000.00 | |
    | `liability.in_transit.EUR` | | 989.09 |
    | `revenue.fee.corridor.EUR` | | 4.85 |
    | `revenue.fee.fx_spread.EUR` | | 6.06 |

    Arc now owes Amina €1,000 less. €989.09 sits in in-transit — committed but not yet paid out — and €10.91 has become revenue in two separately named accounts so it can be reported and reconciled independently.

    If Amina's balance cannot fund the full amount, `reserve` fails on the overdraft floor and the saga stops here, before anything external has been touched.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Swap — a EUR obligation becomes a USDC asset">
    **Service:** Movement → Ledger · `kind: fx`

    The swap converts Arc's EUR obligation into USDC ready for on-chain settlement:

    | Account | Dr | Cr |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | `liability.in_transit.EUR` | 989.09 | |
    | `equity.fx_position.EUR` | | 989.09 |
    | `asset.float.chain.USDC` | 1,072.35 | |
    | `equity.fx_position.USDC` | | 1,072.35 |

    Two currencies, each balancing independently. Neither half references the other — the position accounts are the bridge, and the standing balance they now carry **is** Arc's open FX exposure on this transfer. Until the settle step closes those positions, that exposure sits on the books exactly as it should.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Settle — pick a chain, broadcast, wait for finality">
    **Service:** Movement + `packages/chain`

    `selectChain` scores available chains on settlement time and fee. With a high speed preference this picks **Base**: 2-second blocks, 10 confirmations, a ~20-second settlement window. Polygon would have been cheaper and would have taken 256 seconds.

    The broadcast uses an idempotency key derived from the transfer id, so a retry cannot double-send. Then — and this is the step that is easy to get wrong — the transaction is **re-read and required to be `final`**, not merely submitted without error. A transaction that was submitted successfully but later dropped would still be final in a dishonest sense; this step insists on on-chain confirmation.

    | Account | Dr | Cr |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | `equity.fx_position.USDC` | 1,072.35 | |
    | `asset.float.chain.USDC` | | 1,072.35 |
    | `asset.float.bank.KES` | 138,254.00 | |
    | `liability.in_transit.KES` | | 138,254.00 |

    The gas actually spent posts as a **separate, never-reversed** journal:

    | Account | Dr | Cr |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | `expense.network_fee.ETH` | 0.000018 ETH | |
    | `asset.float.chain.ETH` | | 0.000018 ETH |

    The network fee journal is never reversed because gas was genuinely spent. Reversing it would produce a balanced ledger that misstates a real expense. A `settlement.confirmed` event is published with the chain, transaction hash, confirmation count, and network fee.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Payout — discharge the obligation on the local rail">
    **Service:** Movement · `SimulatedRail` (mpesa)

    M-Pesa is an instant rail with no cut-off window, and it carries the **highest timeout rate** of the six rails Arc simulates. The payout is submitted with Amina's transfer id as the idempotency key — resubmitting returns the original receipt rather than paying twice. [The payout that paid twice →](/stories/the-payout-that-paid-twice)

    | Account | Dr | Cr |
    | --- | --- | --- |
    | `liability.in_transit.KES` | 138,254.00 | |
    | `asset.float.bank.KES` | | 138,254.00 |

    Float down, obligation gone. Arc no longer owes anyone anything on this transfer.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Notify">
    `transfer.settled` is published. The notifications service consumes it and sends Amina a confirmation. Her mother's phone shows an M-Pesa credit for KES 138,254.00.

    Elapsed time: roughly **25 seconds**, dominated by the chain's 20-second finality window.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## The state afterwards

| | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Amina's EUR balance | €1,000.00 | €0.00 |
| Beneficiary's KES | — | KES 138,254.00 received |
| `liability.in_transit.EUR` | 0 | 0 |
| `liability.in_transit.KES` | 0 | 0 |
| `revenue.fee.corridor.EUR` | 0 | €4.85 |
| `revenue.fee.fx_spread.EUR` | 0 | €6.06 |
| `expense.network_fee.ETH` | 0 | 0.000018 ETH |
| Trial balance, every currency | 0 | **0** |

<Note>
Every intermediate account is back to zero. Arc kept €10.91 in gross revenue against a real gas expense, and the trial balance is zero in EUR, KES, USDC, and ETH independently.
</Note>

## What it cost her

€10.91 on €1,000 is **1.09%**, against a World Bank average of **8.78%** for this region. That comparison is fair only up to a point: Arc's figure excludes the cost of the KES float that made instant payout possible, and the last-mile distribution economics are absorbed by M-Pesa rather than eliminated. [Why corridors cost so much →](/primer/why-corridors-cost-so-much) takes that apart honestly.

## If something had failed

Any step after compliance failing triggers compensation — the saga walks completed steps **backwards**, posting the exact opposite journal at each one:

| Failed at | What unwinds |
| --- | --- |
| `reserve` | Nothing — no money moved yet |
| `swap` | Reserve reversed; Amina refunded €1,000.00 exactly |
| `settle` | Swap reversed, then reserve reversed |
| `payout` | Rail recall attempted, then settle → swap → reserve reversed in that order |

In every case the ledger ends balanced, Amina's balance is **exactly** €1,000.00, and every intermediate account returns to zero. The chaos suite asserts this at each of the five failure points.
